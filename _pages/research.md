# Research

Our overarching goal is to explore better ways to understand, interpret, and manipulate visual data using artificial intelligence. We are interested in building models that do not merely recognize images and videos, but can reason about them, generate them, edit them, and interact with them through language.

To achieve this, we develop new generative models, multimodal learning frameworks, and representation learning techniques that integrate vision, language, sound, and physical reasoning. Our work spans foundational modeling questions as well as practical systems for controllable visual generation and understanding.

Our lab brings together researchers with backgrounds in computer vision, natural language processing, graphics, signal processing, and machine learning. We build both conceptual frameworks and scalable systems that allow us to address the questions we find most compelling about visual intelligence.

Here are some of the themes and techniques that we currently work on:

---

## Generative Models for Controllable Image and Video Creation

A central research direction in our lab is controllable generation. We investigate how diffusion models, transformers, GANs, and hypernetworks can be structured to produce images and videos that are editable, interpretable, and aligned with human intent.

Key questions include:

- How can language guide image and video generation in a structured and controllable manner?
- How can motion, style, and content be disentangled for coherent video editing?
- How can generative models respect physical constraints and commonsense reasoning?
- How can panoramic and 360° visual content be generated without distortions?

We explore diffusion-based editing, language-assisted motion planning for video synthesis, spherical transformers for omnidirectional content, and efficient video representations based on hierarchical structures. Our goal is not only to generate realistic visuals, but to make generation controllable and compositional.

---

## Multimodal Learning and Vision–Language Integration

Another core focus of our research is multimodal intelligence. We study how models can jointly reason over images, videos, text, and audio.

Questions we investigate include:

- How do multimodal models generalize compositionally?
- Can models perform few-shot reasoning across modalities?
- How can language-grounded representations improve scene understanding?
- How should we evaluate linguistic, cultural, and temporal reasoning capabilities of large multimodal models?

We develop benchmarks for multimodal few-shot learning, frameworks for evaluating linguistic and temporal grounding, and multilingual multimodal datasets. Our work aims to move beyond surface-level alignment toward deeper cross-modal reasoning.

---

## Low-Light, Event-Based, and Hyperspectral Vision

Visual perception under challenging conditions remains an open problem. We study how event-based sensors, hyperspectral imaging, and low-light imaging can be enhanced using modern deep architectures.

Our research includes:

- Event-guided low-light video enhancement
- Event-based video reconstruction
- Self-supervised hyperspectral image denoising and enhancement
- Spectral modeling for multispectral scene representation

By combining signal processing principles with transformer-based and generative architectures, we aim to build models that can recover structure and semantics from highly degraded or high-dimensional visual inputs.

---

## Representation Learning and Structured Reasoning

Beyond generation and enhancement, we are interested in representation learning for structured visual reasoning.

We explore:

- Object- and relation-centric representations
- Physical commonsense understanding in video generation
- Dataset cartography and curriculum-aware learning
- Behavioral testing of large language models in domain-specific contexts (e.g., healthcare)

Our goal is to understand what current models truly learn, how they fail, and how to design architectures and evaluation frameworks that encourage robust generalization.

---

## Toward Unified Visual Intelligence

Long-term, we aim to build unified models that:

- Integrate vision, language, sound, and motion
- Support controllable and interpretable generation
- Generalize compositionally and across domains
- Operate robustly under real-world sensing conditions

We believe progress in visual intelligence requires both better modeling and better evaluation. Therefore, we contribute not only new architectures but also datasets, benchmarks, and analysis frameworks that help the community measure real progress.
